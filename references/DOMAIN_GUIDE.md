# Research and visualization checks by subject

These are authoring guides, not prebuilt lessons. Every new topic needs actual sources, suitable geography and a reviewed plan.

| Subject | Meaningful spatial question | Visual vocabulary | Accuracy check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Earthquakes | Why do two places face different tectonic settings? | Locations, sourced plate/fault boundaries, schematic relative movement | Do not predict earthquakes or invent fault traces. Source dated event data. |
| Civilizations | How did rivers, terrain and access shape settlement? | Sites, rivers from sourced geometry, approximate teaching regions | State time period. Avoid geographical determinism or claiming one cause explains a civilization. |
| Trade | Why does a route pass through a chokepoint? | Ports, schematic routes, alternate route comparison | Separate conceptual route from live shipping tracks; source distances and traffic figures if used. |
| Climate | How do latitude, elevation, currents or barriers affect a pattern? | Locations, honest regions, schematic flows | Climate is not today's forecast; distinguish observations from explanatory models. |
| Ecosystems | Why do contrasting habitats occur nearby? | Protected sites, elevation/context, sourced or schematic habitat extents | Terrain imagery alone does not establish species presence or current ecosystem condition. |
| Migration | What geography affects movement between places? | Schematic corridors, origin/destination context, dated patterns | Distinguish human/animal movement, measured tracks vs general routes; never imply all populations take one path. |
| Urban development | Why did a city expand or develop around particular features? | Urban locations, sourced dated extents, infrastructure routes | Today's Earth imagery is not evidence of past boundaries. Do not claim historical reconstruction without assets. |

Ask a transfer question where the context changes. Examples: different wind direction, a different river setting, an alternate chokepoint, or a contrasting tectonic setting. Ask the learner to reuse a mechanism, not memorize which colour was correct.

Exclude topics with no useful spatial explanation. If the learner asks for an abstract topic, offer a clearly bounded geographic application instead of pretending the globe makes every subject easier.
